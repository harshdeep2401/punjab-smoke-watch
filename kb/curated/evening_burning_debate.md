# Views reported after the ISRO study

NASA's Earth Observatory (15 December 2025, https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/new-timing-for-stubble-burning-in-india) reported that Hiren Jethva, using the Korean geostationary satellite GEO-KOMPSAT-2A, found most stubble fires now happen between 4 and 6 in the afternoon, later than before, and that monitoring relying only on MODIS or VIIRS misses many of them.

ThePrint (3 December 2025) also reported a dissent. Ravindra Khaiwal of PGIMER Chandigarh had written that claims of farmers evading detection by burning after 2 pm were unsubstantiated, that over 95% of fires are detected in morning passes, and attributed the fall to enforcement and other practices.

The Tribune (6 December 2025, https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/reduced-farm-fire-claims-misleading-farmers-evading-satellites-isro-study/) reported that the government credits a fall in farm fires of nearly 90% to state action plans, residue-management machinery and enforcement.

The question is disputed. This project cannot settle it without ground or geostationary data.
