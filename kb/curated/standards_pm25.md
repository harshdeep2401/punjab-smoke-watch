# Two reference levels for PM2.5

When a briefing says a day is "high", it helps to compare it with two published reference levels. Both are for 24-hour average PM2.5 and are in µg/m³.

- **India, National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS), notification G.S.R. 826(E) of 16 November 2009:** 60 µg/m³ for the 24-hour average. The annual average standard is 40 µg/m³.
- **WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines, 2021:** health-based guideline levels, not legal limits, and much lower than the Indian standard. The 24-hour level is a 99th percentile, so it may be exceeded on 3 to 4 days a year (who.int, "What are the WHO air quality guidelines?").

# How we use them

In our city season summaries we count the number of days with a daily mean above 60 µg/m³. We call this "days above the NAAQS 24-hour level".

Two cautions apply. First, our daily means come from the CAMS model, not from regulatory monitors, so this count is not an official exceedance count. Second, the standards are defined on measured data with specific averaging and data-completeness rules. We apply only the simple threshold to a modelled daily mean.

The WHO level is much stricter than the Indian standard, so almost every day in the stubble-burning season is above it. That level is mainly useful as context.
