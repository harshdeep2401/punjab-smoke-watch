# The seasonal cycle

Punjab grows paddy in the monsoon season and wheat in winter. Paddy is harvested in autumn and wheat is sown straight after. The gap is short: in its 2024 status report to the National Green Tribunal, the State of Punjab said the demand for wheat sowing is concentrated in a limited window of 15 to 20 days, so machines may not be available when farmers need them.

# Why burning happens

Combine harvesters leave a lot of loose straw and stubble in the field. With only a short gap before wheat sowing, some farmers burn the residue because it is a fast and cheap way to clear the field. This is why the satellite fire counts in Punjab rise in October and early November, and why we look at the window from Sep 15 to Nov 30.

# Management options (in general terms)

- **In-situ management:** the residue is handled in the field, for example by machines that sow wheat directly through the straw (such as the Happy Seeder), or by mixing the straw into the soil.
- **Ex-situ management:** the straw is baled and removed from the field, then used for example as fuel in biomass plants and industry, or as fodder or packing material.

Both routes need machines, transport and a buyer or user for the straw. The cost and availability of these are a common part of the policy discussion.
