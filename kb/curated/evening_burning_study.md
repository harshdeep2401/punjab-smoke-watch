# What the study did and found

Singh, Pradhan, Shukla and Pandya of ISRO's Space Applications Centre (Current Science, volume 129, number 10, 25 November 2025) used fire detections from the SEVIRI instrument on the geostationary Meteosat Second Generation satellite, taken every 15 minutes over Punjab and Haryana, for 15 October to 30 November of 2020 to 2024.

They report that peak fire activity moved gradually from about 13:30 IST in 2020 to about 17:00 IST in 2024, and that the daily window of fire activity narrowed. They read this as farmers adjusting to avoid polar-orbiting satellites. Over 2020 to 2024, Suomi NPP VIIRS counts fell steeply while SEVIRI counts showed a slight upward slope.

# Limits to keep in mind

The authors note that geostationary sensors have coarse resolution (2 to 5 km), saturate at lower temperatures and are less sensitive to faint fires. The study covers Punjab and Haryana together, not single districts, and it covers 2020 to 2024. Avoidance as a motive is the authors' interpretation: a satellite shows when heat was seen, not why a fire was lit at that time.
