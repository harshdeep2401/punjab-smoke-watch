# In-situ and ex-situ management

Punjab's status report of 26 November 2024 to the National Green Tribunal says paddy covers about 32 lakh hectares and leaves about 19.7 million tonnes of straw. In-situ management deals with the straw in the field, using seeding machines such as the super seeder to sow wheat. Ex-situ management collects the straw, mainly with balers, and takes it away for industrial and other uses. The state's action plan put in-situ management at 11.50 million tonnes in 2023 and 12.70 million tonnes (estimate) in 2024, ex-situ at 3.66 and 5.96 million tonnes, and fodder at 0.70 and 0.86 million tonnes.

# Machines and custom hiring

The report says 146,540 crop-residue machines, 61,951 of them super seeders, had been provided on subsidy under the Crop Residue Management scheme since 2018-19, including 2,183 balers and 2,039 rakes. Custom Hiring Centres are being promoted so that farmers do not have to buy the machinery and so that each machine is used closer to its capacity.

# Why timing and placement matter

The state says the number of machines is enough for the paddy area by their field capacity, but almost all the demand falls in the wheat sowing window of 15 to 20 days, so a machine may not be available when a farmer wants it. Machines also wear out, and some are discarded every year. These are the state's own statements and have not been checked independently here.
